## Informal Description

This class focuses on the foundations of programming and software
development to solve problems - in this case focused on the needs of
ECE majors. We will use C in this class, which is most directly useful
going forward for low level and embedded programming in your later
courses. To build your programming skills, you must practice – it is
only by reading code, writing code, and working through the stages of
software development that you can learn and improve.  Programming is a
creative exercise, and programming problems often have multiple
correct solutions. This class will require you to think critically and
creatively about how best to apply your skills to a particular task.

This course does not assume that you have any prior programming
experience. But, like many other Rose-Hulman courses, CSSE 120
requires that you keep up with each day's work. After a few class
sessions, you will have quite a bit of programming experience, or
you will be behind!

exercises, so missing is a bad idea.  Contact me via email to arrange

My expectation is that you come to class by the time I take
attendance.  If you do not arrive on time, it is your responsibility to
see me *during class* and ensure your attendance record is updated.
Students who are chronically late or very late (e.g. more than 20
minutes late) may have their lateness treated as absences.

students to cite any and all sources of help received in completing
coursework. This practice not only fosters a culture of honesty and
transparency but also prevents misunderstandings that might otherwise
escalate to formal proceedings. Students should also be aware of what
is appropriate help on homework assignments (link below). To ensure
fairness and responsibility, any instances of suspected misconduct
will be handled through the CSSE Integrity Committee.

CSSE Integrity Committee for review (link below). The process includes an initial review of the evidence
by the committee, a time for students to explain or admit to potential
misconduct, and potentially a hearing to examine the circumstances and
evidence. Students are encouraged to continue their studies and engage
with the course material and instructor normally throughout the
investigation.

### Avoiding misconduct in CSSE 120

Exams are to be done individually, with no communication with anyone
other than your instructor and their delegates. Each exam will have
explicit rules for what external resources you may use for what parts
of the exam.

For all work other than exams, getting help and working with others is
encouraged. Just follow these four rules for all work that you turn
in as your own:

#### Rule 1:  Attempt the problem yourself first (or with your group in a group exercise).

It is NEVER right to begin by copying someone else's solution.

If you find a complete solution on the internet to the very same
exercise that you are doing, you may NOT use that solution in any
way. Just ignore it! You may, however, use any solution that your
professor supplies, e.g. from a video, from live coding, on a
whiteboard, or sending it to you directly.

#### Rule 2:  Make a sincere effort to understand the concepts behind the problem on which you are getting help.

If you do NOT understand your own code, it is NOT yet your own work;
instead, ask your instructor or a Learning Center tutor for help!

#### Rule 3:  Unless specified otherwise (as on exams), you may use the following resources:

Any materials that your instructor supplied.

For example, you may copy an example from code that we supplied when
you are working on your own code, modifying the example as needed.

WARNING: Be cautious about doing such copying too much. There is a lot
to be said for typing (rather than copying) your code, to help develop
“muscle memory”.

Any explanations (but NOT examples of code) from any source (not
LLMs), including humans.

WARNING: Be cautious about getting help from anyone other than your
instructors and Learning Center tutors.  They are specially trained to
help you learn, not merely solve the problem.

If you use an outside source to help with an assignment, you must
clearly cite that source, and state what you used it for.

#### Rule 4:  All submitted work must be your own.  LLMs are forbidden.

Copying code or answers from the internet or from another student is
not allowed. Similarly, giving your solutions to another student is
prohibited. **Using an LLM to help in any way with any graded
assignment in this class is cheating.** While LLMs ARE useful tools
and used professionally, in this case they are detrimental to you.  Do
not use them.

You are allowed to discuss the homework and other parts of the class
with other students. Such discussions about ideas and concepts are not
cheating (as long as specific solutions are not provided), whereas the
exchange of written answers or code is cheating. The best way to work
with another student is to discuss only the concepts of a problem, and
not the actual solution. If you discuss a problem with another
student, give credit where credit is due by making a note such as “the
following idea was developed jointly with Grace Hopper,” or “the
following idea is due to Alan Turing.” Similarly, if you use any
reference materials (other than the course materials) to solve a
problem, please cite the referenced material.  When collaborating,
beware of the difference between helping and hurting yourself and
other students. In brief, you can help another student by teaching
them, and you can hurt them by giving them answers that they should
have worked out for themselves. The same applies to tutoring and
getting help from the instructor. Correct answers on homework that you
don’t understand will not help you on a timed exam.

## Course Content

Some details required to be in the syllabus.

### Catalog Description

CSSE 120 Introduction to Software Development

An introduction to programming with an emphasis on problem
solving. Problems may include visualizing data, interfacing with
external hardware or solving problems from a variety of engineering
disciplines. Programming concepts covered include data types,
variables, control structures, arrays, and data I/O. Software
development concepts covered include testing, debugging, incremental
development, understanding requirements, and version control systems.

Prerequisites: None.

### Course Objectives

Students who successfully complete this course should be able to:

Analyze, explain, and apply fundamental programming concepts including:

- Syntax and semantics
- Types, variables, expressions, and assignment
- Branching control structures
- Loops, both definite and indefinite
- Functions, parameter passing, user-defined functions
- Input and output
- Sequences and strings
- Modularity and structural decomposition

Design, implement, debug and test small programs for solving problems
motivated by real-world interests, using the above concepts and modern
software engineering practices including (where appropriate, and at an
elementary level):

- An appropriate integrated development environment with version control
- Coding to a specification
- Iterative enhancement
- Pair programming
- Test-first programming
